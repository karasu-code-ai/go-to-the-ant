The double bridge path optimization, emergent

Goss et al. 1989. The nest and the food are joined by two branches of different length. No ant measures anything — yet the colony converges on the shorter path over time. Ants that take the short branch return sooner, so they re-lay pheromone sooner; the nonlinear choice p ∝ (c+φ)² amplifies that lead. This is the optimization the open-plane forager can't show — because there, there is no shorter route to choose.

Watch the short branch brighten and the ants shift onto it. Set the ratio to 1× (equal branches) and neither wins — there's nothing to optimize. Slide the ant count down and the colony sometimes locks onto the long branch instead: the shortcut is a statistical result, not a guarantee — exactly the stochastic outcome Goss reported, and more reliable the larger the colony.
